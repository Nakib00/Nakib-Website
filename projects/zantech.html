The ZAN Tech Story

Robotics Education STEM Kits Since 2020

ZAN Tech didn't start as a company. It started as a fix for a problem Md Hana Sultan Chowdhury and I kept running into as students at Independent University, Bangladesh.

Every semester ended in a Tech Fest, and the lab courses we'd just finished each had their own final project — some robotics, some pure software — that every student across different courses had to actually build to compete. The problem was never the idea. It was that most students in those classes had no real way of knowing what parts they even needed to build the thing their course was asking for — which sensor, which motor driver, which chassis, in what combination.

Hana and I already knew robotics well enough between the two of us, so we started putting together complete parts packages for each course's project — everything a student needed for their specific build, bundled into one order, with free consultancy on how to actually put it together. We called the whole thing ZAN Tech.

The way we ran it became a routine that shaped the whole first stretch of the business. Our classes ran Sunday to Wednesday, so that stretch of the week was spent taking orders from basically the entire university — word got around fast once people realized we were handing them a complete, no-guesswork package instead of a shopping list. Every Saturday, Hana would come in from Uttara and I'd come from Bashundhara, and together we'd head to Old Dhaka to buy the actual components. We'd get back late, and spend that same night packing every order by hand. Sunday morning, we delivered. Order, source, pack, deliver, repeat — that cycle is where ZAN Tech actually started earning, and where the name stopped being a joke between two students and became a real business.

From packing bags of components for classmates, the next step was obvious: take robotics, programming, and AI/ML to students who'd never had access to any of it at all. We started running seminars and workshops at schools, colleges, and universities, funded entirely out of ZAN Tech's own earnings and completely free for the institution — the initiative we later named Project Uddipon.

Around 2025–2026, ZAN Tech got its first real office. We now run three to four structured courses — robotics, AI, Python, block coding, and 3D design — teaching students from Class 1 all the way through Class 12. And we're finally building the thing that started this whole line of thinking in the first place: our own robotics kits. Existing options like Lego or micro:bit are priced completely out of reach for most Bangladeshi families, which quietly pushes kids out of robotics before they ever get a real chance at it. We're building kits meant to actually be affordable here — several, like the ZAN School STEM & Robotics Starter Kit and the Line Following Robot Kit, are already live on our store.

You can see the current version of all of this — courses, workshops, and the parts store the whole thing grew out of — at zantechbd.com and store.zantechbd.com.
